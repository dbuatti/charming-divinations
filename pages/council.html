Council of the Root

A circle, one element, ninety minutes

The shape of a session

Four movements

The same spine every time, so the element can be the thing that varies.

01 · Opening

Blessing & grounding

An opening blessing — Archangel Michael, the spiritual guides, the divine, and the grandmothers of the four directions. Then a crystal bowl, and breath to let go of what was carried in.

02 · Teaching

The element

What the element is, what it governs, and the myth or story that holds it. Taught intuitively — the frame is set, the words are not scripted.

03 · Ritual

A working

Something simple and repeatable to take away — a two-glass pour for water, a breathing practice for air, a candle working for fire.

04 · Circle

Meditation & sharing

A guided meditation, then the circle: what was seen, what surfaced, what people want to carry forward. The part that is never scripted.

Session one · Water

Change you don't fight

Water was introduced as the foundational element — connected to birth and hydration, and to the body as a water vessel. It governs movement, change, and the capacity to adapt to large shifts without fixating on what has been lost.

The teaching leaned on the Inuit goddess Sedna as a warning: there are currents that will drown you if you swim against them. The Nile floods were offered as the counterweight — destructive-seeming events that nourish progress anyway. And a line that runs through all of it: once the intention and the trajectory are set, you are not in control of the outcome.

The two-glass working

All the water in the left glass. Then, each day through the lunar cycle from the dark moon to the full moon, pour a little across, affirming the change you are working towards. Simple enough to do at a kitchen table, and specific enough to be worth doing.

What the circle brought back

The guided meditation took the group down withered wooden stairs to a private sand-dune beach, into calm water, and — for the first time — underwater. What surfaced in the sharing circle: mermaids pointing to a fire kingdom, a wartime shipwreck and a lost coin, a coral city and dolphins arriving on the far side of fear. And a bowl, recurring for one person, that turned out to be waiting for them the whole time.

Session notes

What to bring

  • Headphones, if you have them
  • A glass of water
  • A dark bowl, for scrying
  • A candle and incense, if you have them
  • Ninety minutes, and a little quiet around it

A note on the practice

At the close of the first session Linda said: "I don't know how I feel about council." It is worth keeping that on the page. A practice that can be questioned out loud is a practice that is still alive.
