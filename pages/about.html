About

Teaching that leaves room for you

Linda Webster is a kinesiology practitioner and the teacher behind the Council of the Root. What unites both is a belief that people already know more than they have been told.

How she teaches

Linda's teaching is intuitive and largely improvised. She prepares the frame and the handouts, then lets the session breathe — spirit guides the content, and it changes from week to week. Her classes have always run for ninety minutes, and in person she has taught two sessions in a day.

What she is careful about is the layer of intermediaries. Linda is direct about the misinformation that circulates in spiritual and witchcraft circles: people should be able to connect directly with the divine rather than routing that through priests, gurus or a prescribed lineage. And she is equally direct that a teaching which sits badly with you is probably not yours to take on.

A taste, not a doctrine

When it comes to readings, Linda's stated aim is to genuinely help people, not to perform the role of a predictive figure. She describes her preference as giving people a taste of what is, and then letting them choose their own path. That philosophy runs through the whole practice — the courses, the ritual circles, and the one-to-one work.

What the training involved

Linda's kinesiology training ran through the College of Complimentary Medicine: anatomy and pathology, client intake assessment, postural balance and correction, and clinic hours with real people and real complaints.

The clinical work is demanding in a particular way. An intake is not a form to fill in — it is a careful reading of someone's history, symptoms, triggers and baselines, followed by an honest assessment of what you are seeing, what the risks are, and what you are not going to attempt. It is the same discipline that shows up later in a ritual circle: attend properly, then act from what is actually there.

The clinical side

Alongside this, Linda works in kinesiology — the assessment of postural stress, pain and energy levels, and the balances and corrections that follow from them. She has completed client intake assessments and practical balances through the College of Complimentary Medicine, and has assessed other students in that work.

The two strands meet in the elemental framework: water as hydration and adaptation, air as breath and clarity, fire as transformation, earth as grounded knowing, spirit as integration. The Council of the Root is where those threads are pulled together into a single live practice.

The through-line

Water, and what came after

The first Council session, in three movements.

The teaching

Change you don't fight

Water as the foundational element — movement, change, and learning to endure hardship without looking at it as lack. The Inuit goddess Sedna as the warning against swimming against the current.

The ritual

Two glasses

A simple working for change: all the water in the left glass, then a little poured each day through the lunar cycle, affirming the change you want.

The meditation

Down the stairs

White light forming a protective shell, then down withered wooden stairs to a private dune beach — and the discovery that you can breathe underwater.
